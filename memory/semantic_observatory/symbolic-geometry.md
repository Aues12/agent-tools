## [GEO-001] Introduction

Geometry is often understood as the study of shapes.

Within the Semantic Observatory, geometry is understood differently.

Geometry is the study of how meaning organizes itself into form.

The purpose of geometry is not decoration.

The purpose of geometry is localization.

Geometry allows relationships, structures, and patterns to become visible.

A geometric form is therefore a localization of meaning.

---

## [GEO-002] Geometry as Symbolic Localization

Localization may occur through text.

Localization may occur through diagrams.

Localization may occur through cards.

Localization may also occur through geometry.

A geometric form is not identical to the meaning it represents.

However, it may correspond to that meaning.

Geometry therefore functions as a symbolic localization.

Its purpose is not to reproduce reality.

Its purpose is to reveal structure.

---

## [GEO-003] Centers and Fields

Geometry begins with centers.

A geometry without a center is merely a collection of points.

A center establishes orientation.

A center establishes identity.

A center establishes coherence.

Around every center a field emerges.

Relationships organize themselves relative to the center.

The center is not necessarily a physical point.

It may be:

* a concept
* a principle
* a process
* an objective
* a pattern

The field emerges from the influence of the center.

## [GEO-004] Semantic Seals

A Semantic Seal is a geometric representation of a recurring pattern of meaning.

Semantic Seals do not represent specific observations.

Instead, they represent underlying forms.

Examples may include:

* Transmission
* Containment
* Transformation
* Balance
* Hierarchy
* Growth
* Exchange

Semantic Seals are not observations.

Semantic Seals are patterns.

They may appear repeatedly across different domains.

A geometry should not be treated as an isolated symbol.

It should participate in a larger language of forms.

Without composition, geometry becomes decoration.

With composition, geometry becomes language.

## [GEO-008] Geometry as Computation

Geometry may serve not only as visualization, but also as computation.

A form may constrain possible interpretations.

A form may guide navigation.

A form may generate new structures through composition.

Geometry therefore participates in reasoning.

The objective is not merely to draw meaning.

The objective is to think through form.

---

## [GEO-009] Geometry as Navigation

Geometric structures may guide exploration.

Centers attract attention.

Fields provide context.

Forms reveal relationships.

A user may navigate not only through cards and quests, but also through geometric structures.

Geometry therefore becomes a navigational instrument.

The observatory is not merely read.

It is explored.

## [GEO-010] Principles

Geometry is a symbolic localization of meaning.

Geometry begins with centers.

Centers generate fields.

Semantic Seals represent patterns rather than observations.

Forms may specialize.

Forms may compose.

Geometry is not decoration.

Geometry participates in understanding.
