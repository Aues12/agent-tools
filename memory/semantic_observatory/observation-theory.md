## [OBS-001] Introduction

Observation Theory begins with a simple proposition:

Knowledge may be represented as observations connected through relationships.

Rather than treating knowledge as isolated documents, Observation Theory treats knowledge as a network of observations.

An observation may describe:

* a concept
* a person
* a theory
* a software component
* an event
* a pattern
* a relationship

The form is secondary.

The observation is primary.

---

## [OBS-002] Observation Units

An Observation Unit is the smallest meaningful object within a knowledge system.

Examples include:

* a function
* a class
* a scientific theory
* a historical event
* a philosophical concept
* a person

Observation Units do not exist in isolation.

Their meaning emerges through relationships.

---

## [OBS-003] Relationships

Observations become knowledge when connected.

Examples include:

* influences
* extends
* contains
* depends_on
* contradicts
* corresponds_to
* evolves_into

Relationships transform collections into structures.

---

## [OBS-004] Knowledge Graphs

A knowledge system may be understood as a graph of observations.

Documents become clusters.

Disciplines become regions.

Theories become pathways.

Meaning emerges from both nodes and connections, it emerges from The Journey.

---

## [OBS-005] Navigation

Knowledge is not merely stored.

Knowledge is explored.

A user may move:

* between observations
* between disciplines
* between levels of abstraction

Navigation is therefore a fundamental part of understanding.

---

## [OBS-006] Compression

Observation Units act as compression mechanisms.

Large amounts of information may be condensed into a smaller number of meaningful observations.

Compression should preserve relationships.

Information without structure becomes noise.

---

## [OBS-007] Principles

Observation precedes interpretation.

Relationship precedes classification.

Knowledge emerges through connections.

Navigation is part of understanding.

Compression should preserve meaning.
