## [SC-001] Introduction

Semantic Cards are navigable representations of knowledge.

They are not the knowledge itself.

They are structured interfaces through which knowledge may be explored.

A card gathers information around a single conceptual center.

Its purpose is not merely storage, but navigation and understanding.

---

## [SC-002] Observation Units and Cards

Observation Units and Semantic Cards are related but distinct.

An Observation Unit represents the object of observation.

A Semantic Card represents that observation within a navigable knowledge system.

For example:

Newton is an Observation Unit.

A card describing Newton is a Semantic Card.

The observation and the card correspond to one another, but they are not identical.

---

## [SC-003] The Card as Localization

A Semantic Card is a localization of an Observation Unit.

Just as source code localizes meaning in text, a card localizes an observation within a structured interface.

Different card designs may represent the same observation.

The observation remains constant.

The localization may change.

---

## [SC-004] The Card as Compression

Cards act as compression mechanisms.

Large amounts of information may be condensed into a smaller and more navigable form.

Good compression preserves meaning.

Poor compression destroys context.

The purpose of a card is not to reduce information, but to preserve what is essential.

---

## [SC-005] The Card as Interface

Cards act as interfaces between users and observations.

A user rarely interacts with an observation directly.

Instead, they interact with its card.

The card therefore serves as a bridge between knowledge and exploration.

---

## [SC-006] The Card as Portal

A card is not merely a container.

A card is a portal.

Through a card one may travel:

* upward toward abstractions
* downward toward implementations
* outward toward related observations
* inward toward greater detail

A card therefore participates in both localization and navigation.

---

## [SC-007] Card Relations

Cards gain meaning through relationships.

Examples include:

* contains
* influences
* extends
* depends_on
* corresponds_to
* evolves_into

A card without relationships is isolated.

A card with relationships becomes part of a larger structure.

---

## [SC-008] Card Collections

Individual cards rarely exist alone.

Cards form collections.

Collections may represent:

* disciplines
* projects
* theories
* systems
* libraries

A collection provides context for the cards it contains.

Cards provide detail for the collection they inhabit.

---

## [SC-009] Principles

Observations are primary.

Cards are localizations.

Cards compress without replacing.

Cards connect through relationships.

Cards enable navigation.

Cards serve understanding.
